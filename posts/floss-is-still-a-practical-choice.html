FLOSS is still a practical choice

Why Free/Libre Open Source Software still shapes how I choose tools, work with communities, and think about the web.

Aug 1st, 2026 By Ferox Filed under FLOSS

Free/Libre Open Source Software is not only a philosophical preference for me. It is also a practical one.

I like knowing how the system works, where the constraints are, and whether I can keep the work alive without asking permission from a platform vendor.

That matters more over time, not less.

The practical side of FLOSS

Open source is useful because it gives you options.

Those options include:

  • being able to inspect what the software is doing
  • adapting workflows to real needs
  • moving between providers or environments with less lock-in
  • participating in communities instead of only consuming products

That does not magically make every open source tool better. But it does create a healthier starting point.

Community matters too

I have described myself as a Fedora Ambassador, and that is part of the same story.

Technology is never only about tools. It is also about the communities around those tools:

  • how knowledge gets shared
  • how newcomers are treated
  • how people collaborate
  • how ideas stay open to modification and reuse

That side of the work matters to me as much as the code itself.

Why it still belongs on a personal site

When I talk about FLOSS here, I am not trying to repeat slogans from an earlier era of the internet.

I am talking about daily decisions:

  • what I build with
  • what I recommend
  • what I trust to stay maintainable
  • what kind of web I want to help preserve

For me, the answer is still clear. Open tools, open standards, and open communities remain the most durable path.
