HydePHP fits the way I like to publish

Why HydePHP is a pragmatic middle ground between a handwritten static site and a heavier application stack.

Aug 1st, 2026 By Ferox Filed under Tech

HydePHP sits in a useful spot for me.

It keeps the output static, but it does not force every page into the same content model. That matters for a personal site because the homepage, archive, and article pages usually need different levels of control.

The tradeoff is good

With HydePHP, I can use:

  • Markdown for normal posts
  • Blade for pages that need custom structure
  • PHP-native configuration for routing, metadata, and navigation

That gives me more leverage than a pure Markdown stack, without pushing the site into the complexity of a full dynamic application.

Good defaults, replaceable layers

The other advantage is that Hyde does not trap you in its defaults. You can override the views, adjust the build pipeline, and keep the project understandable.

That is exactly what a personal site should optimize for.
